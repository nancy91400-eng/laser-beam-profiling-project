Academic Documentation & Physics Reference

Complete theoretical background, equations, and analysis for B.Tech mini project study

1. Introduction

A laser resonator supports discrete transverse electromagnetic field distributions known as spatial modes or TEMmn (Transverse Electromagnetic) modes. Unlike ideal plane waves, real laser beams exhibit finite transverse spatial extent and specific intensity patterns governed by resonator geometry, aperture diffraction, and gain medium symmetry.

Laser beam profiling is the measurement and analysis of a beam's spatial irradiance distribution I(x, y) across a plane perpendicular to propagation. Understanding spatial profiles is essential because beam focusability, power density, and optical coupling efficiency depend strongly on the active transverse mode.

2. Project Objectives

  • Simulate and visualize 2D and 3D transverse intensity profiles of laser beams using pure HTML5 Canvas and JavaScript.
  • Compare the fundamental Gaussian mode (TEM00) with higher-order Hermite-Gaussian modes (TEM01, TEM10, TEM11).
  • Demonstrate how beam waist (w0) and reference intensity (I0) control spot size, peak irradiance, Rayleigh range, and beam divergence.
  • Provide accurate horizontal and vertical intensity cross-sections alongside normalized and absolute metrics.

3. Working Principle

Inside a stable rectangular or spherical-mirror laser cavity, light bounces back and forth between mirrors. Only electromagnetic field patterns that reproduce themselves in phase and amplitude after one round trip can resonate efficiently. Under the paraxial Helmholtz equation, these self-consistent cavity solutions form the orthogonal family of Hermite-Gaussian modes.

The indices m and n count the number of zero-intensity nodal lines along the horizontal (x) and vertical (y) axes respectively. Higher indices introduce phase reversals (π radians) between adjacent lobes, producing dark nodal planes where electric field amplitude cancels to zero.

4. Mathematical Model (Simple Explanation)

At the beam waist plane (z = 0), all modes share a Gaussian envelope multiplied by Hermite polynomials Hm and Hn. To ensure fair comparison at constant total beam power P = ½πw0²I0, the intensity equation is:

Imn(x, y) = [ I0 / (2m+n · m! · n!) ] · [ Hm(√2 · x / w0) · Hn(√2 · y / w0) ]² · exp( −2(x² + y²) / w0² )

Plain-Language Variable Guide

  • w0 (Beam Waist): The radius where the fundamental TEM00 intensity drops to 1/e² (≈ 13.53%) of its peak. Larger w0 spreads energy over a wider spot.
  • I0 (Reference Intensity): Peak intensity of the fundamental TEM00 beam at the center (x = 0, y = 0).
  • exp(−2(x² + y²)/w0²): The bell-shaped Gaussian decay factor that keeps energy confined near the beam axis.
  • Hm(u) (Hermite Polynomials): Shape functions creating lobes and dark nodal lines: H0(u) = 1 (no nodes) and H1(u) = 2u (one node at u = 0).

Key Beam Propagation Equations

  • Rayleigh Range: zR = πw0² / λ — distance over which the beam cross-sectional area doubles.
  • Far-Field Half-Angle Divergence: θ = λ / (πw0) — narrower waists diverge faster due to diffraction.
  • Beam Quality Factor: Mx² = 2m + 1, My² = 2n + 1 — measures how close a mode is to the diffraction limit (M² = 1 for TEM00).
  • TEM00 FWHM: Full Width at Half Maximum = w0√(2 ln 2) ≈ 1.1774 w0.

5. Types of Spatial Modes

TEM00 (Fundamental Gaussian)

Single smooth central peak (m = 0, n = 0) with M² = 1. Produces the smallest possible focused spot size and lowest divergence for a given beam waist.

TEM01 (Vertical Hermite-Gaussian)

Two symmetric lobes stacked vertically along y (m = 0, n = 1) separated by a horizontal dark nodal line at y = 0. Peak intensity drops to 2e−1I0 (≈ 73.6% of I0).

TEM10 (Horizontal Hermite-Gaussian)

Two symmetric lobes arranged horizontally along x (m = 1, n = 0) separated by a vertical nodal line at x = 0. Peak occurs at x = ±w0/√2.

TEM11 (Four-Lobe Mode)

Four bright quadrant peaks (m = 1, n = 1) divided by perpendicular nodal lines at x = 0 and y = 0. Peak intensity is 4e−2I0 (≈ 54.1% of I0) with Mx² = My² = 3.

6. Applications

  • Optical Fiber Coupling & Telecom: Single-mode fibers require TEM00 profiles for maximum coupling efficiency.
  • Precision Laser Micromachining: TEM00 achieves minimal kerf width and high irradiance in cutting, drilling, and lithography.
  • Optical Tweezers & Atom Trapping: Higher-order modes (e.g., TEM01/TEM10 or Laguerre-Gaussian superpositions) create multi-site or structured optical potentials.
  • Laser Cavity Alignment & Diagnostics: Observing TEM10 or TEM11 patterns in a beam profiler helps engineers diagnose mirror tilt or aperture misalignment.

7. Results and Discussion

From the simulation, increasing the mode order (m + n) redistributes the same total optical power P across multiple off-axis lobes. Consequently, peak irradiance drops from 100% of I0 (TEM00) to 73.58% (TEM01/TEM10) and 54.13% (TEM11), while the effective beam width and M² factor increase.

8. Conclusion

This simulation demonstrates the direct link between transverse electromagnetic mode indices, Hermite-Gaussian intensity distributions, and physical beam quality metrics. While TEM00 delivers unmatched peak power density and diffraction-limited focusing, higher-order spatial modes provide distinct multi-lobe geometries suited for specialized optical trapping, sensing, and resonator diagnostics.
